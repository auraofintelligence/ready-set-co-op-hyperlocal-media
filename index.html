People and stories first.

Ready S.E.T. Co-op is the training and work pathway. Hyperlocal Media is the public storytelling layer. Together they help locals see what is happening, learn useful tools, and get paid for practical community work.

Local context

Small island, complex work.

North Stradbroke Island / Minjerribah is not a generic suburb. It has three main townships, a strong visitor economy, deep Quandamooka cultural context, older residents, small businesses, community groups, service gaps and seasonal pressure.

The blend

A co-op without visibility is invisible. Media without work is noise.

The co-op side helps with onboarding, training, compliance, work-readiness, volunteering, paid local services, event crews and future employment pathways.

The media side turns that activity into useful local signal: news, events, weather notes, ferry and safety updates, artist and musician spotlights, film-maker callouts, grant releases, project explainers and Community Honour Board stories.

It also needs a culture and translation layer: plain-English updates, visitor-friendly explainers, Jandai language pathways where appropriate and approved, museum stories, environmental science, and careful local material literacy about quartz, rutile, ilmenite, monazite and other island minerals.

The pipeline should be simple at the front and smart underneath: capture once, check the facts, then reformat the same story for phones, websites, newsletters, social platforms, venue screens and future local dashboards.

Likely first path

Start with useful local services, then grow.

The first practical build is a repeatable local content pipeline: collect trusted updates, keep community context attached, then publish the right version for each platform or device.

01Intake local signal

News tips, events, weather notes, ferry changes, community notices, grant releases and project updates.

02Verify and sort

Check dates, names, permissions, cultural context, source links and what needs a human editor before publishing.

03Translate and explain

Prepare visitor-friendly versions, plain-English summaries, approved Jandai pathways, museum notes and cross-cultural context.

04Package the story

Create the short article, caption, poster copy, voice note, interview prompt, reel script or event listing from the same source material.

05Reformat everywhere

Autonomously adapt content for phones, websites, newsletters, social feeds, vertical video, venue screens and accessibility needs.

06Close the loop

Feed outcomes into grant evidence, paid pathways, supporter records and the Community Honour Board when a project gains backing.

Local knowledge layer

Share culture, science and material possibility without extracting the island.

Ready SET Media can help explain what makes Minjerribah distinct: Jandai language and cultural learning where permission exists, museum and family-history stories, weather and ecological observation, and the science of local sand, quartz and heavy minerals.

The point is not to dress up old extraction for export. It is to build local resilience literacy: what these materials are, why they matter, what can be made from them responsibly, and how future manufacturing, repair, batteries, ceramics, sensors or education projects could serve the island first.

Next practical build

One training offer. One media rhythm. One local project.

The first version can stay small: a paid help desk at markets, one monthly local media update, one beginner AI/social media workshop, and one visible community project page. That proves the pattern before legal structure and formal co-op machinery get heavy.
