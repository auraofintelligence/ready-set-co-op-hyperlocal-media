Plain version

People and stories first.

Ready S.E.T. Co-op is the training and work pathway. Hyperlocal Media is the public storytelling layer. Together they help locals see what is happening, learn useful tools, and get paid for practical community work.

Local context

Small island, complex work.

North Stradbroke Island / Minjerribah is not a generic suburb. It has three main townships, a strong visitor economy, deep Quandamooka cultural context, older residents, small businesses, community groups, service gaps and seasonal pressure.

2,100+usual residents in the local research range, before peak visitor surges
24%aged 65 and over in the research notes, so simple access and patient training matter
15.9%Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander usual residents in the research notes
3 townsDunwich / Goompi, Amity / Pulan and Point Lookout / Mooloomba need linked but localised support

Stats are working notes from the Stradbroke Island Research Project and should be checked before formal grant submission.

The blend

A co-op without visibility is invisible. Media without work is noise.

The co-op side helps with onboarding, training, compliance, work-readiness, volunteering, paid local services, event crews and future employment pathways.

The media side turns that activity into public memory: short videos, local interviews, event coverage, project explainers, business spotlights, training clips and grant stories.

The landing page stays light because most people will arrive from Strange but True. The deeper pages hold the bigger architecture.

Likely first path

Start with useful local services, then grow.

People do not need to understand the full ecosystem on day one. They need help with real things: tech, events, social media, paperwork, training, grant stories, project pages and local visibility.

01Meet locally

Markets, events, tech help, music, short conversations and word of mouth.

02Fix real problems

Phones, laptops, forms, social media, Meta Business Suite, simple AI tools, websites and community admin.

03Train and document

Turn repeated help into simple checklists, clips, workshops and local training pathways.

04Publish the useful bits

Share public updates, interviews, event recaps, project pages and grant-friendly evidence.

05Build paid pathways

Pay locals for useful work, make volunteer work visible, and keep records clear.

06Connect support

When a project gets real backing, link it back to the Community Honour Board pathway.

Next practical build

One training offer. One media rhythm. One local project.

The first version can stay small: a paid help desk at markets, one monthly local media update, one beginner AI/social media workshop, and one visible community project page. That proves the pattern before legal structure and formal co-op machinery get heavy.
