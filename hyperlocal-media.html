A possible local pathway

Stories and useful public information

Make it clear. Check it properly. Share it where it helps.

Local media can be as simple as a clear event notice, a short interview, a project update or a two-minute video. The job is to help people understand what is happening without turning guesses into facts or private business into public content.

Good local media sounds like a helpful neighbour.

It tells you what happened, what is coming, who checked it, what is still unknown and where to find the proper source.

The new joined-up home

The Minjerribah Screen & Media Network carries the bigger public pitch.

This Ready SET page explains the skills-and-work pathway. The newer network site brings film, local news, training, noticeboards, screens and possible gathering places together in one community-facing picture.

It is still a proposal. It does not claim to be an operating newsroom or to represent any local organisation.

One story, handled properly

The human checks stay attached from start to finish.

01Listen

What does the person want to share, and who is it for?

02Check

Confirm names, dates, sources, safety, permission and what is still unknown.

03Make

Prepare the clearest useful version: words, sound, photos, video or a simple page.

04Approve

A responsible person checks the final draft before it leaves the workbench.

05Share

Put it on the channel that reaches the right people without oversharing.

06Keep the trail

Save the source, consent boundary, correction path and useful project history.

What people could make

Learn by making

Every useful story can teach a real skill.

A beginner can start with one job: hold the phone steady, ask a clear question, write the date correctly, add captions, check a name or prepare the public version of a notice.

The next time, they can do a little more. That is the Ready SET part: real work becomes a gentle learning path, not a test people have to pass before they are allowed to help.

Some things need more care

Permission is not a tick-box at the end.

Cultural material

The right cultural authority and context must guide what can be recorded, edited or shared.

Children and young people

Use clear consent and safeguarding. A public event does not make every image public property.

Private details

Keep phone numbers, addresses, health details, raw contacts and sensitive project notes out of public content.

Emergencies and safety

Repeat checked official information with its source and time. Do not replace the responsible authorities.
